## Research platforms

### Foundry120

![foundry120 screenshot](/projects/foundry120.webp)

Foundry120 is a research operating system for translational science. It keeps sample tracking, clinical data, omics and documents in one governed environment, so a study team can follow a sample from the freezer box to an analysis-ready cohort.

It started life as G-Trac, a QR-code sample tracker I built for the MUSIC IBD study. It has since grown to support multiple studies and sites, and now has three layers: G-Trac for sample operations, a central research data layer, and Helix, an AI assistant for grounded querying, cohort discovery and analysis. I designed and built it, and lead its development. I wrote about why we built it in [Building the Research Operating System We Needed](/posts/building-the-research-operating-system/).

[www.foundry120.com](https://www.foundry120.com)

### ChatIBD

![chatibd screenshot](/projects/chatibd.webp)

ChatIBD is an AI assistant for clinicians who manage inflammatory bowel disease. It answers clinical questions from current IBD guidelines and cites the passages it used, with verified dosing information for adult and paediatric regimens. Clinicians can flag an answer or escalate a conversation to staff, and every answer can be traced to the version of the guideline corpus behind it.

I built ChatIBD and lead the project. I write about its development on the [ChatIBD blog](https://www.chatibd.com/blog).

[www.chatibd.com](https://chatibd.com)

## Clinical studies

I helped set up these studies as part of Prof Gwo-Tzer Ho's [Gut Translational Research Group](https://www.gla.ac.uk/schools/infectionimmunity/staff/gwotzerho/theguttranslationalresearchgroup/) at the University of Glasgow, and built their websites.

### MUSIC Study

![music study screenshot](/projects/musicstudy.webp)

MUSIC (with its paediatric arm, Mini-MUSIC) is a multi-centre, prospective cohort study investigating mitochondrial DAMPs as biomarkers of gut inflammation in IBD. Since 2020 it has recruited newly diagnosed adults and children across Glasgow, Edinburgh and Dundee, with a target of 250 patients, and followed them from active disease onwards. The study website has information for participants, our latest results, and audio summaries of our research made with our patient involvement group.

[www.musicstudy.uk](https://musicstudy.uk)

### MARVEL Study

![marvel study screenshot](/projects/marvelstudy.webp)

MARVEL is a UK multi-centre phase 2 randomised controlled trial of MitoQ, a mitochondria-targeted antioxidant, in adults with ulcerative colitis. It plans to recruit 250 patients, and its primary outcome is clinical response and remission at week 24. The study website explains who can take part and lists the participating centres and study team.

[www.marvelstudy.uk](https://marvelstudy.uk)

## Community

### ScotRight

![scotright screenshot](/projects/scotright.webp)

ScotRight (Scottish Trainees' Research in Gastroenterology and Hepatology) is a trainee research network that launched in 2022. It gives gastroenterology trainees across Scotland an easy way to join multi-centre research and audit projects. Membership is open to anyone interested, especially pre-specialty trainees. I was part of the founding team and set up the network's web services, including membership, the newsletter and consent management.

[www.scotright.com](https://scotright.com)

### Gut Liver Glasgow

Gut Liver Glasgow is a website for gastroenterology and hepatology clinicians, trainees and researchers in the West of Scotland. Its trial finder is an early prototype: a clinician describes an anonymised patient, and an AI assistant suggests recruiting studies the patient might be eligible for. The site also lists education events such as the West of Scotland GI Teaching Day. I built it as part of a project with my GI colleagues in Glasgow.

[gutliverglasgow.com](https://gutliverglasgow.com)

## Teaching

### Software Engineering for Science

Software Engineering for Science is a course for new members of our research team who have no coding background, such as registrars starting out-of-programme research. It covers the software engineering practices that make research code reliable and reproducible. The companion reference site is open to everyone.

[Course materials](https://shaunchuah.github.io/software_engineering_for_science)
